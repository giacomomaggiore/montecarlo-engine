# Probability and estimation

Monte Carlo estimates a distribution of outcomes conditional on a model. It does not turn historical data into a forecast with certainty.

## Sampling error

For $N$ independent simulated outcomes, the standard error of an estimated mean declines approximately with $1 / \sqrt{N}$. Reducing simulation noise by half therefore requires roughly four times as many paths.

## Correlation and covariance

Covariance records joint movement in return units; correlation standardizes that movement to a scale from $-1$ to $1$.

$$
\rho_{ij} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(r_i, r_j)}{\sigma_i\sigma_j}
$$

Correlation describes ordinary linear co-movement. It does not by itself guarantee that a model captures joint market stress.
